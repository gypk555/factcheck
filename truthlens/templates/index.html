        Check the picture itself

        TruthLens judges the words in your screenshot, not the photo. Old photos reused with a new, false story are common, so search for where the picture appeared before.

        1. Save the photo part of the post (crop out the caption).
        2. Upload it to a reverse image search: Google Lens or TinEye.
        3. If it appeared years earlier or in another country, the post is likely misleading.

        How TruthLens decides, and where it fails

        TruthLens turns your text into word and word-pair scores (TF-IDF) and feeds them to a Logistic Regression model trained on {{ "{:,}".format(meta.get('train_examples', 0)) }} examples from the WELFake news dataset. Every highlighted word's colour is its exact share of the final score, so the explanation is the model's real reasoning, not a guess.

        It judges how a text is written, not whether it is true. It has no knowledge of events. A carefully written lie can pass, and a true story written in a sensational style can be flagged.
